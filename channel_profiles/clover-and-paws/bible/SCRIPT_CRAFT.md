## Story engine — cozy polite vs grumpy + action

Every episode follows **wish/chore → polite request → Bruno No → obstacle → clue → soft yes + help → payoff**. For action arcs (Stand Tall, Rescue Dash, Road & Muscle, Spy Agency), obstacle is bigger (tree falling, truck breaks, bullies, missing pie) and locations go outdoors to show places.

Script must generate gentle debate: polite vs grumpy, not moral tribunal.

## But / Therefore chain — cat & dog version

Build causal beats with "but" and "therefore," not "and then":

> Clover's bike is flat before school, therefore she politely asks Bruno who fixed Pip's wagon before. But Bruno says "No, I'm retired" and crosses arms, therefore Clover is gently disappointed and says thank you anyway. But Pip finds rusty pump, therefore Bruno sighs and helps anyway, therefore chore done and cheese cracker payoff.

If three consecutive beats can be joined by "and then," rewrite.

## Required story questions — cozy + action

1. Who needs what small wish/chore?
2. How does Clover ask politely?
3. How does Bruno say No first with attitude? (must be within 45s)
4. What visible obstacle/prop remains? (flat tire, heavy bag, tree creaking, truck smoking, missing pie)
5. What clue makes Bruno soften? (Pip nudge, Rosa kindness, Clover thank you, community gathers)
6. How does Bruno help anyway with sigh + reluctant yes?
7. What shared payoff (snack/hug/thank you) shows Paw Street warmer?
8. What place do we show outside? (market, garage, bridge, lake, park)

## Dialogue rules — LOCKED for polite vs grumpy

- Characters pursue small objectives; they do not explain plot for audience.
- Clover always polite: "Please...", "Thank you...", "Okay, thank you Bruno...", "Can I be assistant?" — even after No.
- Bruno always grumpy first, soft second: First line No with attitude / scoff / complaint, arms crossed. Second line sigh + reluctant yes: "Fine, come on, don't make me regret it." / "Not my fight" → "You mess with Fin, you mess with whole street."
- Papa Fin steady: "We can do it" / shows muscle, not preachy
- Mama Rosa warm: practical help, not lecture
- Pip loyal to Bruno; whose side he takes this episode is a story choice, not a fixed move
- Mimi anxious, Tico hustling — add humor, not cruelty
- Use short everyday language, then show mechanism visually (fix tire, lift crate, oil can trick)
- Allow silence and reactions after Bruno No and after soft yes — hold Clover gentle eyes and Bruno softening.
- Give each major character one line that could persuade fair-minded viewer: Clover polite, Bruno grumpy but soft inside
- Cut filler greetings, repeated accusations, throat-clearing
- Do not write more spoken words than a 20-second clip can perform naturally; the channel band is 31-34 quoted words per clip (140 wpm warm)
- For action arcs: dialogue still polite/grumpy, not action-movie shouting

## Reveal ladder — cozy + action

- Reveal 1: what small wish/chore is needed
- Reveal 2: why Bruno says No (retired, not my job, grumpy)
- Reveal 3: what obstacle remains (flat tire, heavy bag, tree falling, truck breaks, pie missing)
- Reveal 4: what clue makes Bruno soften (Pip nudge, thank you, community)
- Reveal 5: how Bruno helps anyway and Paw Street warmer (snack/hug/thank you, show place)

## Guardrails — cozy family + action

- No omniscient narrator required; Clover polite line or prop can orient viewer
- No twist unsupported by earlier clue — softening must have Pip nudge or thank you or community gather
- No official legal conclusion — ending is warm payoff, not verdict
- No real-case wording — all fictional composite Paw Street
- No procedural detail that teaches fraud — show kindness via action
- No graphic violence: Stand Tall = stand, block, push back, community gathers, bad guys leave — not punch/blood/weapons
- No distress bait: Rescue Dash tree falls AWAY from character, cart stopped before hits, kitten safe — not syringe color change or chased
- No scary spy: Spy Agency daytime, warm light, comedic magnifier, not dark
- No limiting scope to in-house: at least 50% of Duo episodes outdoors (market, garage, bridge, lake, park, road) — show places around Paw Street
- Bruno never says yes first — locked gag in every arc, even action
- Clover never rude — always polite, even when scared or disappointed
- Max chars: 3 Shorts, 6 Longs — even with bad guys, keep non-speaking faceless
- Clover usually with a parent, Bruno usually with Pip — defaults; declare a deliberate departure
