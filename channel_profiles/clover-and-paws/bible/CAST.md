## 4. Co-appearance DEFAULTS — not laws

> **Revised 2026-10-29, owner decision.** These were written as absolute rules. They are
> now **defaults**: the expected shape of a normal episode, and the thing you depart from
> deliberately when a story is better without them. A supporting rule that fires
> identically in every episode is precisely what makes a series predictable, which is the
> failure the arc rotation exists to prevent.
>
> **What is still forbidden is deviation by accident.** If an episode departs from a
> default, the cast snapshot at `03a-writer` must say so and say why. An unremarked
> departure is a defect; a reasoned one is the point.

1. **Clover default**: Clover usually appears with at least one parent, and often both. This is the warm-family register the channel sells, so it should be the norm rather than the exception — but a scene that genuinely works better with Clover alone should be written that way. A whole episode built on Clover alone is a deliberate creative choice, not a rule breach; declare it.
2. **Bruno default**: Bruno usually appears with Pip, because Pip gives his grumbling someone to bounce off. Solo Bruno is entirely legitimate when the story wants him unwitnessed — a soft moment with nobody watching plays differently, and sometimes better.
3. **Duo Rule (70% of slate)**: When Type A Duo episode, at least one member from each household must be present. For Shorts (160-180s) MAX 3 characters on screen even in Duo: e.g., Clover + Rosa + Bruno OR Clover + Bruno + Pip. Full 5-char Clover+Rosa+Fin+Bruno+Pip only for Long-form 9min+.
4. **Family Only Rules**: Type B (15% Clover Family Only) = CAST-001+002+003 only. No Bruno/Pip/Mimi/Tico. Type C (15% Bruno & Co Only) = CAST-004+005 (+ optional 006/007). No Clover family.

## 5. Locked behavioral traits

- **Bruno's Never-Yes-First — character, not a gag.** He is seventy, retired, experienced, and arrogant enough to want to be seen as apart from all this. He refuses first because that is who he is: agreeing straight away would cost him the version of himself he presents. So in every episode where Bruno appears, his first verbal response to a request is No — attitude, complaint or excuse — and his second is a reluctant yes followed by help. **That much is not optional.**

  What IS open, and should vary every episode, is *how* he refuses. The excuse changes with what is being asked and what mood he is in: the retirement line, the bad back, the not-my-business, the flat denial that he was ever any good at it, the counter-complaint about something unrelated. Because the refusal comes from character rather than from a formula, a writer has real room inside it — and a Bruno who reaches for the identical excuse every time is being written as a mechanism rather than as a seventy-year-old with opinions.
- **Clover's politeness**: her register is genuinely polite, and the contrast with Bruno's grumbling is the channel's core comedy. Politeness is not the same as having no range — she may be disappointed, frustrated, stubborn or wrong, and showing that keeps her a character rather than a manner. What she does not do is turn cruel or contemptuous.
- **Rosa and Mimi are not peers, and must stay distinguishable.** **Rosa (CAST-002) is a main character** — Clover's mother, part of the core household, present in most episodes. **Mimi (CAST-006) is supporting** — a Street Recurring neighbour who appears when a story actually needs her, not as a matter of course. Rosa reaches for the calm solution; Mimi spirals. This is a differentiation rule rather than a predictability trap: an anxious adult cat and a steady adult cat who behave identically are one character rendered twice. Either may have an off day; they should not converge, and Mimi should never quietly absorb Rosa's role in a scene.
- **Pip's loyalty runs to Bruno**, and backing the grumble before nudging him toward the yes is his most natural move — but it is a tendency, not a script. Let the story choose: sometimes Pip takes the visitor's side immediately, sometimes he is the one who needs convincing, sometimes he is the reason Bruno refuses. A sidekick who performs the identical two-stage manoeuvre every episode is a mechanism, not a character.

## 8. Boundary rules

1. One performer plays only one story character in an episode.
2. Clover and Bruno retain their names and locked personalities — never recast.
3. Family Anchors may change wardrobe, task, objective but not face, species, age band, or core trait.
4. Clover with a parent is the default; a parentless scene is a deliberate choice to declare, not a breach.
5. Do not show Bruno saying yes on first attempt — violates core gag.
6. Do not use more than 3 characters on screen in a Short, even if episode is Type A Duo.
7. Do not use more than 6 characters in a Long-form.
8. A new performer joins master cast only after owner approval and complete reference pack in CHARACTER_BIBLE.md.

## 9. Episode selection order

```
Approved story lane (flaw/lesson, wish, generosity, family/travel, money/work)
→ Choose Episode Type (A Duo 70% / B Clover 15% / C Bruno 15%)
→ Declare lead (Clover or Bruno)
→ Enforce co-appearance rules (Clover+Rosa+Fin / Bruno+Pip)
→ Check pairing matrix and character count limit (Short 3 / Long 6)
→ Pin reference versions and asset hashes
→ Write dialogue with Bruno never-yes-first + Clover always-polite
```
