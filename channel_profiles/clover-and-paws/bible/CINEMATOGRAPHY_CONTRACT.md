## Purpose

Dialogue is cozy family engine, but video must not feel like two generated animal heads taking turns. Camera, blocking, and small chore inserts should reveal kindness, task progress, and Bruno's grumpy-to-soft shift every 20-40 seconds.

## Core rules — LOCKED for cat n dog

1. Establish screen direction before coverage — preserve left/right eyelines: Clover left polite, Bruno right grumpy for Duo.
2. Give each character camera-relative facing note: Clover looks up politely to Bruno, Bruno looks down slightly with scoff.
3. Cut on change in knowledge/politeness/grumpiness, not merely because line ends. Cut when Bruno says "No" first, hold reaction on Clover polite.
4. Hold reaction shots long enough to register Clover's gentle disappointment and Bruno's softening.
5. Do not generate a continuous 3-min Short as one clip. Build fixed 20-second performance units and bridge with controlled inserts, room tone, J/L cuts.
6. Use locked or slow push for dialogue; reserve gentle handheld or slight movement for chore (fixing bike, cooking, carrying bag).
7. Always show Bruno's physical tell for never-yes-first: arms crossed on "No", sigh + uncross + soft eyes on reluctant yes.

## Required shot vocabulary — cozy family

| Shot | Primary use |
|---|---|
| Chore close-up | Cold-open task: flat bike tire, dirty dishes, laundry pile, shopping list |
| Kitchen two-shot | Clover + Rosa/Fin continuity, warm |

| Silent reaction close-up | Clover polite disappointment after Bruno says No; Bruno softening — no lip movement |
| Hero-prop insert | Bike pump, spoon, coin, bag, lawnmower part — must appear by 0:10 |
| Street/threshold shot | Transition between houses, Paw Street walk, emotional decompression |
| Payoff symmetry shot | Both families together, equal weight, shared snack/hug/thank you |

## Coverage target

### For Shorts 160-180s (proven growth driver)
- 40-50% dialogue: polite request + Bruno No + polite still + Bruno soft yes
- 20-30% chore/prop inserts: bike, dishes, bag
- 15-20% reactions: Clover gentle eyes, Bruno sigh
- 10% opening + closing payoff
- Avoid >15 seconds without change in composition, prop state, or grumpy-to-soft shift
- MAX 3 characters on screen even if Duo type

### For Longs 9-11 min target 9:30
- 35-45% dialogue and cross-house talk
- 25-35% chore/repair/trip
- 10-15% reactions and pauses
- 10-15% opening, transitions, closing payoff
- Avoid >25 seconds without change
- MAX 6 characters

## Clip-unit template

Every clip is a fixed 20 seconds. For every clip specify:

- the cut point
- who speaks and in what order (CLOVER polite, BRUNO grumpy 70y)
- starting composition and camera height (kitten low, old dog slightly high)
- camera-relative facing and eyeline target
- one primary physical action (Clover holds bike, Bruno crosses arms)
- exact dialogue that fits naturally and respects locked traits: Clover always polite, Bruno first line No with attitude, second line reluctant yes
- diegetic sound: tap drip, porch creak, bag rustle, coin drop
- continuity-in and continuity-out frame state (tire flat -> inflated)

## Post-production continuity — cozy

- Use separately rendered warm room tone across cuts, not cold office tone
- Prefer J-cuts into chore and L-cuts out of polite request
- Insert all readable text (shopping list) in post, keep simple
- Match fur tone, exposure, black level before adding warm cream/wood grade
- Use single restrained transition system: hard cut, short audio bridge, or prop wipe. No random glitch packs, no brass financial grade
- Keep lighting bright warm daylight for kitchen/porch, not dark navy tribunal

## Rejection triggers

- Ambient animals/humans draw attention or mutate fur
- Non-speaker produces lip movement
- Room tone changes abruptly
- Music masks dialogue or manufactures tension/fear
- Story-critical prop sound before visible action
- Bruno says yes on first attempt — violates core gag
- Clover rude or arrogant — violates polite lock
- Kitchen looks dirty/scary or porch threatening
