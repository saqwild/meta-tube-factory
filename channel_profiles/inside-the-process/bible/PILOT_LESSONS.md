# Lessons from September 2026 visual experiments

These notes summarize an earlier conversation's three Veo test clips, made
before either production profile (LTX-2.5 or this LTX-2.5 profile) was
chosen. They are non-canonical feasibility evidence. This repo does not
depend on Downloads paths or import those files as LTX renders. Audio was not
assessed.

## Explorer approaches injury

Recognizable ivory/orange capsule and consistent overall identity. Headlamps
direct attention to the injury. The result tended toward polished CGI and a
spacious tunnel. Platelet action was too small for a clear explanation.

Lesson: use the establishing shot briefly, then move to a process close-up.
Keep immersed environments visibly fluid and check material/lighting realism.

## Crowded platelet close-up

More readable texture and platelets, but many were already attached in the
starting image. The causal change was subtle.

Lesson: start BEFORE the action and reduce foreground entities. Do not
claim an attachment demonstration from a static already-attached cluster.

## One-platelet attachment

A visible approach, contact and held position made the action clearer.
The already-attached platelet still shifted/enlarged, and spreading was
not convincingly demonstrated.

Lesson: maintain fixed contact anchors for attached entities. Only say
"spreads" when flattening/spreading actually occurs. Match the narration to
the accepted visible action or revise through the owning stage.

## What this does not establish

No LTX-2.5 or LTX-2.5 quality/cost comparison, complete episode, medical
validation, viewer demand, monetization or stable cross-episode character
lock has been proven by these three clips. Use these lessons to design this
channel's own first LTX-2.5 pilot (`04h`), not to skip it.
