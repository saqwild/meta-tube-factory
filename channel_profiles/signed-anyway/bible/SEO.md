This is not a real-case documentary channel. Do not publish named-company exposés, portray recognizable real executives, imitate public voices, or imply that invented scenes are leaked footage, court records or true events.

## 3. Live-research and data-integrity rule

The previous named-case validation table is retired because it measured the wrong channel and returned upload dates for `0/30` results. Its freshness and stale-share conclusions are invalid.

### Volume rule

YouTube ordinary search does not expose reliable public monthly query volume. Record **sample volume** only as the number of results actually analyzed. Record monthly volume only when captured from a named provider such as YouTube Studio Research, Google Trends set to YouTube Search, or an approved keyword platform; log geography, date range and source.

### Required two-pass validation

| Pass | Upload-age filter | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Evergreen SERP | None | Measure the proportion uploaded within 12 months versus older than 12 months |
| Active competition | Last 12 months | Measure current publishing activity, packaging quality and accessible competitors |

For each primary query, record:

- exact query, capture date and timezone;
- country, language, sort mode and upload-age filter;
- inclusion and exclusion criteria;
- results sampled and dates successfully parsed;
- median views, duration bands and Shorts share;
- uploaded within 12 months and older than 12 months;
- exact-query relevance and top-three-channel concentration;
- weak or outdated titles/thumbnails and source URLs;
- monthly volume plus provider, or `not available`.

Require valid dates for at least 90% of the sample; prefer 30/30. Missing dates are `invalid`, never zero. Do not combine metrics from differently filtered or differently normalized samples.

## 4. Title contract

### Preferred formulas

- **[Person] + [hidden act] + [specific consequence]**
- **The [role] had [time limit] to explain [specific loss]**
- **[Technology/decision] did [harm]. Who is responsible?**

### Rules

1. Put the human decision and concrete consequence in the first 55–65 characters where practical.
2. Use a specific amount, deadline or affected-person count only when it is internally verified and appears on screen by 0:10.
3. Keep titles at 70 characters or fewer where practical; YouTube permits more, but mobile truncation weakens the promise.
4. Questions must have two genuinely defensible answers.
5. Lead with the case, never `AI generated`, episode number, channel name or series name.
6. Do not use a real company/person as discoverability bait.
7. Never claim `true story`, `based on real events`, `leaked`, `real footage`, `court transcript` or `documentary` for these fictional cases.
8. Do not expose the reversal or official verdict.

## 5. Description contract

The first two visible lines must sell the episode. The disclosure stays last so it remains clear without replacing the hook.

```text
[One-sentence consequence-led hook using the primary phrase naturally.]

[Two short sentences establishing the hidden decision, financial stake and opposing duties without revealing the reversal.]

YOUR VERDICT
A — [Exact Option A from the episode]
B — [Exact Option B from the episode]
Comment A or B and explain which duty matters most.

Watch every case in The Moral Ledger: [master playlist URL]
More [Money Between Us / Corporate Reckonings / Future Verdicts]: [subseries URL]

#FinancialDrama #MoralDilemma #[one episode-specific hashtag]

Fictional disclosure: This is an original fictional dramatization created using synthetic performers, voices and environments. No real person, company or event is depicted.
```

### Description rules

- Repeat the primary phrase only when natural; never stack keyword variants.
- Describe the fictional case, not the production model.
- Add chapters for story navigation without naming the reversal.
- Affiliate or sponsor disclosures must be conspicuous and placed where legally/platform-required; they do not replace the final fictional disclosure.
- Do not imply individualized legal, tax or financial advice.

## 6. Comment and end-screen engine

### Pinned comment

```text
YOUR VERDICT
A — [Exact Option A]
B — [Exact Option B]

Reply A or B, then name the duty that decided it for you.
```

The pinned wording must match the final on-screen verdict exactly. Do not add a creator verdict.

### End screen

Reserve the final 20 seconds for:

- the visible Option A / Option B prompt;
- one **Best for viewer** video element;
- Subscribe.

Keep the verdict copy outside the clickable element footprints. The dramatic story resolves before the end screen begins; only the viewer's judgment remains open.

## 7. Tags and hashtags

Tags are secondary metadata. Use 10–15 relevant comma-separated tags and stay below YouTube's 500-character field limit.

### Channel-level tag bank

`financial betrayal stories, financial infidelity story, corporate ethics drama, corporate fraud story, moral dilemma short film, AI ethics story, inheritance dispute story, financial drama, money betrayal, dialogue driven story, ethical dilemma story, cinematic short film, The Moral Ledger, Signed Anyway`

### Episode stack

1. exact primary phrase;
2. two close natural variants;
3. two to four mechanism/consequence terms;
4. one format term;
5. relevant subseries;
6. `The Moral Ledger` and `Signed Anyway`.

Use three to five hashtags. `#FinancialDrama` and `#MoralDilemma` are required; add one to three episode-specific hashtags. Do not repeat the same word merely to fill the field.

| Oxblood | Maximum 3% of frame; never a large red panel, balance or glow |
| Output | 3840×2160 16:9 master; compressed upload below 2 MB when practical |
| Tests | Critical content inside central 80%; legible at 10% scale |
| Overlay reserve | Lower-right 25% width × 18% height left clear for duration/interface overlays |

Keep the bottom-left number at least 10% inward from the left and bottom edges. Ban arrows, circles, red rings, shocked-face-plus-explosion layouts, more than three words, episode numbers, fake news lower-thirds and unreadable generated documents.

### Title-thumbnail split

The title carries the event and consequence. The thumbnail carries the emotional or evidentiary contradiction.

Pilot example:

- Title: **He Saved 2,000 Jobs by Lying to the Bank**
- Thumbnail: Marcus on the right, certificate and empty locker; `2,000` bottom-left; exact spoken line **WHO PAID?** on the left.

Create two materially different concepts before upload. Use YouTube's native testing when available. Judge click-through rate together with average view duration and traffic source so misleading curiosity is not rewarded.

## 9. Keyword-to-episode workflow

1. Start with a human decision and irreversible consequence.
2. Assign exactly one primary pillar and one relevant subseries.
3. Generate three to six plain-language queries a viewer might use for that mechanism.
4. Run both required YouTube research passes and log filters.
5. Exclude unrelated results, non-target languages and clips that do not match the intended format.
6. Choose one primary phrase based on relevance—not an unsupported volume claim.
7. Write three title options before scripting; confirm each can be honestly paid off.
8. Put any title/thumbnail amount and the central consequence on screen by 0:10.
9. Write the opening dialogue so the decision and scale are clear by 0:45.
10. Compile description, tags, chapters, verdict wording and Shorts after picture lock.
11. Upload corrected captions; ensure money, roles and financial mechanisms are spelled consistently.
12. Record the final package and research log under the episode ID.

## 11. Shorts funnel rule

Every Short must create an incomplete question that the related long-form episode resolves.

1. 0:00–0:02 — consequence image and accusation.
2. 0:02–0:18 — one fact supporting Side A.
3. 0:18–0:30 — one contradiction supporting Side B.
4. Final 3–5 seconds — point to the full evidence and ask A or B.

Never reveal the reversal, final evidence or creator verdict in a Short. Attach the full long-form episode through YouTube's **Related Video** field. Description and pinned-comment links are supporting routes, not substitutes for Related Video.

## 13. Post-publish analytics

Review at 48 hours, 7 days and 28 days. Compare only reasonably similar traffic sources and episode types.

| Question | Metric | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Did packaging earn attention? | Impressions and CTR by Home, Suggested, Search and Subscriptions | Diagnose title/thumbnail by traffic source, not blended CTR alone. |
| Did the promise match? | Retention at 0:10, 0:30 and 0:45 | Early loss suggests unclear consequence, slow setup or packaging mismatch. |
| Did the story sustain? | Average view duration, percentage viewed and retention at first reconstruction/reversal | Locate pacing, exposition or evidence failures. |
| Did the verdict create participation? | Verdict comments per 1,000 views and A/B split | Measure judgment engagement, not merely total comments. |
| Did the channel create a second view? | End-screen click rate, playlist starts and returning viewers | Test the recurring-format moat. |
| Did the audience fit improve? | Suggested neighbors, search terms and subscriber conversion | Confirm adjacency to financial drama, ethical dilemmas and cinematic shorts. |

Do not set universal “good” thresholds before four comparable long-form uploads. Use Month 1 to establish Signed Anyway's own baseline, then change one major packaging variable at a time.
