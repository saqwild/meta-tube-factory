# Niche Decision

## Final selection

**Financial & Ethics Tribunal drama**, delivered as cinematic 8–11 minute fictional cases with a 9:30 target. The repeatable show is **The Moral Ledger**; the audience hears the evidence and chooses between two defensible verdicts.

**One-sentence value proposition:** Signed Anyway turns money, power and betrayal into cinematic moral cases where every side has a reason—and the viewer gives the verdict.

## What was merged

| Candidate idea | Final role |
|---|---|
| Financial & Ethics Tribunal / The Moral Tribunal | Core episode engine |
| Financial Infidelity / Money Betrayal Theater | Largest launch pillar; relatable and comment-rich |
| Corporate Collapse / Corporate Ethics / Fraud Dialogue | Premium business case family and sponsor adjacency |
| Near-Future Sci-Fi Moral Dilemmas | **Future Verdicts** subseries; discovery and originality |
| Crisis Reconstruction | Cross-pillar visual device; reconstruction-forward corporate cases use the **Event Special** tag inside Corporate Reckonings |

The rejected alternative was a channel limited to reconstructions of real adjudicated fraud. It offers search demand, but increases sourcing, defamation, likeness, fair-use and accuracy risk while weakening the audience-vote mechanic. Real financial concepts may inform the fiction; launch stories must not map cleanly to identifiable people or companies.

## Market gap

Existing supply clusters into documentary fraud essays, personal money confrontations, AI short films, and analog-horror incident reports. The opening is a **premium, repeatable financial drama** combining:

- a recognizable consequence in the first 20 seconds;
- character-led testimony instead of voice-over exposition;
- visual evidence/reconstruction to prevent courtroom fatigue;
- two morally credible sides;
- a final audience vote that drives comments and repeat viewing.

## Content allocation

| Pillar | Share | Job |
|---|---:|---|
| Personal money betrayal | 40% | Relatability, emotion, comments |
| Corporate fraud and ethics | 40% | Authority, higher-value audience, sponsors; includes reconstruction-forward Event Specials |
| Future Verdicts | 20% | Differentiation, shareability, creative range |

Crisis Reconstruction is not a fourth pillar or playlist. It is a visual storytelling device available to every case family. When the crisis event dominates a corporate episode, package it as **Corporate Reckonings — Event Special**; approximately 10 percentage points of the total channel mix may use that label.

## Risk decision

The model must never be the product promise. Viewers should remember the dilemma, characters and verdict—not the generator. LTX-2.5 is the technical core: an open-weights release rendered locally, so no per-output display permission stands between a finished episode and global YouTube publication. Scripts, reference packs, test renders and public release may all proceed once the gates in `COMPLIANCE_GATE.md` pass.
