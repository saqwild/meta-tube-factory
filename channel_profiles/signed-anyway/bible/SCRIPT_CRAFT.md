# Script Craft Contract

## Story engine

Every episode follows **decision → concealment → consequence → evidence → reversal → verdict**. The script must generate genuine disagreement, not merely delay an obvious answer.

## But / Therefore chain

Build causal beats with “but” and “therefore,” not a sequence of “and then” events:

> The bank was about to freeze credit, **therefore** Marcus signed the certificate. **But** the certificate moved the risk to workers and depositors, **therefore** the examiner asks who consented to his gamble. **But** the board had hidden a rescue offer, **therefore** responsibility becomes shared rather than simple.

If three consecutive beats can be joined by “and then,” rewrite the sequence.

## Required story questions

1. Who trusted whom?
2. What was hidden, approved, signed or transferred?
3. What did the decision-maker believe would happen?
4. Who benefited first?
5. Who paid later?
6. What fact changes the viewer’s initial judgment?
7. What are the two strongest verdicts?

## Dialogue rules

- Characters pursue objectives; they do not explain the plot for the audience.
- Questions should expose knowledge, choice, benefit or duty.
- Use short financial language, then show the mechanism visually.
- Allow silence and reactions after consequential facts.
- Give each major character one line that could persuade a fair-minded viewer.
- Cut filler greetings, repeated accusations, throat-clearing and recap dialogue.
- Do not write more spoken words than a 20-second clip can perform naturally; the channel band is 32-36 quoted words per clip.

## Retention structure

| Window | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Specific consequence, face/prop, unanswered question |
| By 0:45 | Decision, amount and affected person/group are clear |
| Every 30–45 sec | New evidence, objective, contradiction, location, composition or sound perspective |
| 3:15–5:15 | Visual reconstruction answers “how” |
| 5:15–6:30 | Reversal changes responsibility |
| Final 25 sec | Two concise verdict options; direct comment prompt |

## Reveal ladder

- **Reveal 1:** what was done.
- **Reveal 2:** why it appeared necessary.
- **Reveal 3:** who absorbed the hidden cost.
- **Reveal 4:** what another party knew or concealed.
- **Reveal 5:** the unresolved duty conflict the viewer must judge.

## Guardrails

- No omniscient narrator required; Evelyn or evidence graphics can orient the viewer.
- No twist unsupported by an earlier clue.
- No official legal conclusion.
- No real-case wording, names, dates, documents or recognizable sequences in launch fiction.
- No procedural detail that meaningfully teaches fraud.
