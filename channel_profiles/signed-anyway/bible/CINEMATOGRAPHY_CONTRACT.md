# Cinematography Contract

## Purpose

Dialogue is the engine, but the video must not feel like two generated heads taking turns. Camera, blocking and evidence inserts should reveal new information every 20–40 seconds.

## Core rules

1. Establish screen direction before coverage; preserve left/right eyelines through the entire exchange.
2. Give each character a camera-relative facing note in the shot plan.
3. Cut on a change in knowledge, power or emotion—not merely because a line ends.
4. Hold reaction shots long enough to register the consequence.
5. Do not generate a continuous ten-minute scene. Build fixed 20-second performance units and bridge them with controlled inserts, room tone and J/L cuts.
6. Use locked or slow movement for testimony; reserve faster moves for reconstruction and discovery.

## Required shot vocabulary

| Shot | Primary use |
|---|---|
| Consequence close-up | Cold-open damage: termination notice, deed, red balance, empty locker |
| Evidence-table two-shot | Power relationship and spatial continuity |
| Examiner over-shoulder | Precise question and eyeline anchor |
| Witness three-quarter close-up | Dialogue performance with stable facial reference |
| Silent reaction close-up | Moral cost; no unintended lip movement |
| Hero-prop insert | Signature, transfer, contract clause, timestamp |
| Reconstruction wide | Causal event or operational consequence |
| Corridor/threshold shot | Transition and emotional decompression |
| Verdict symmetry shot | Equal visual weight to both choices |

## Coverage target for 8–11 minutes (target 9:30)

- 35–45% testimony and cross-examination.
- 25–35% evidence/reconstruction.
- 10–15% reactions and pauses.
- 10–15% opening, transitions, closing and verdict prompt.

These are editorial targets, not quotas. Avoid more than 25 consecutive seconds without a change in composition, evidence state, sound perspective or character objective.

## Clip-unit template

Every clip is 20 seconds. For every clip specify:

- the cut point;
- who speaks, and in what order;
- starting composition and camera height;
- camera-relative facing and eyeline target;
- one primary physical action;
- exact dialogue that fits naturally;
- diegetic sound;
- continuity-in and continuity-out frame state.

## Post-production continuity

- Use separately rendered room tone across cuts.
- Prefer J-cuts into evidence and L-cuts out of testimony.
- Insert all readable evidence text in post.
- Match skin tone, exposure and black level before adding the ink/brass grade.
- Use a single restrained transition system: hard cut, short audio bridge, or evidence-screen wipe. No random glitch packs.
