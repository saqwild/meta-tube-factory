Status: draft at 01-ideation. Every fact here is a research target

## You + everyday action + time

You put a pale, heavy ball of dough in a warm corner, walk away, and
come back to find it doubled. The episode follows your loaf from the
mixing bowl to the moment you slice it.

- Pillar: Inside Materials.
- "You" anchor: the one who meets and uses the material - you bake it,
  then you eat it.
- Clock: the dough's own timeline, mixing to cooling (about 3 hours,
  shown compressed; time compression disclosed on screen).

## Master metaphor

The dough is a foam city under construction. The gluten network is the
scaffolding, yeast are the workers inflating thousands of tiny balloons,
and the oven is the heat wave that locks the whole city solid. The
Explorer enters the foam city and reports from inside its walls.

## Arc (12-clip demo grid)

- Hook (clip 1): your dough doubled while you were gone - how?
- Ordinary process (2-4): mixing, kneading, the gluten net forms.
- Struggle (5-7): gas pressure versus the stretching net; shaping.
- Climax (8-9): oven spring and crust set, Explorer inside the foam city
  as it locks solid.
- Objection (10): "But wait - is the yeast still alive in my bread?"
  (sourced answer at 02a).
- Practical + caveats (11): what this explains about store-bought versus
  home loaves; what we simplified (scale, timing, visibility).
- Callback (12): back to your sliced loaf; closing question.

## Surprise beat (candidate, to be sourced at 02a)

A single loaf contains millions of gas bubbles, and most of the rise in
the oven happens in the first minutes - before the crust sets. Placed
before the climax; leads the Short.

## Voices

- Explorer (CAST-001): helmet-radio lines only, the viewer's questions.
- Control (VOICE-001): off-screen, speaks to "you" throughout.
- Outside clips carry only the "you" figure (CAST-002), never the
  Explorer in the same frame.
