## COV01 - composition

Crumb is a set foam: gas bubbles held in a gelatinized starch and gluten
matrix; crust is its dried, browned outer layer. Bubble size distribution
is what makes crumb soft or dense. Sources:
https://science.howstuffworks.com/innovation/edible-innovations/bread.htm
https://phys.org/news/2023-11-gluten-complex-chemistry-fluffy-goods.html

## COV02 - raw materials

Flour brings starch and gluten-forming proteins; water hydrates them;
yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) ferments sugars to carbon dioxide and
ethanol (ethanol bakes off); salt tightens gluten and moderates
fermentation. Yeast arrives dry and dormant in a packet and activates in
warm water and dough. Sources:
https://science.howstuffworks.com/innovation/edible-innovations/bread.htm
https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/resources/yeast (via benchmark B02
source list; to be confirmed at 02a)

## COV03 - preparation

Mixing hydrates flour; kneading aligns and links gluten proteins into
long elastic strands - the net that traps gas. Without a developed net,
carbon dioxide escapes and the loaf stays dense. Sources:
https://science.howstuffworks.com/innovation/edible-innovations/bread.htm
https://phys.org/news/2023-11-gluten-complex-chemistry-fluffy-goods.html

## COV04 - transformation

Fermentation: yeast converts sugars to CO2; bubbles inflate the elastic
gluten network like thousands of tiny balloons - this is the rise. In
the oven, trapped gas and steam expand further (oven spring), yeast is
killed by heat, starch gelatinizes, and the foam sets into crumb.
Sources: https://science.howstuffworks.com/innovation/edible-innovations/bread.htm
https://www.pizzablab.com/learning-and-resources/baking/dough-baking-processes/

## COV05 - conditions

Warmth speeds fermentation, cold slows it; bakers use time and
temperature to trade speed against flavour. Baking stages by internal
temperature: yeast activity peaks then dies off around 55-60C, wheat
starch gelatinization runs roughly 65-82C, crust browning (Maillard
reaction) develops above about 140C at the surface. Exact figures to be
locked in the 02a claim ledger with qualified sources. Sources:
https://dev.bakerpedia.com/processes/thermal-profiling/
https://www.sustainweb.org/realbread/articles/jul21-bread-science-101/

## COV06 - quality control

Doneness is judged by crust colour, internal temperature (commonly
about 88-99C for many loaves - to qualify at 02a), and a hollow sound on
tapping; under-proofing, over-proofing, and weak gluten give dense or
collapsed crumb. Sources:
https://dev.bakerpedia.com/processes/thermal-profiling/
https://www.sustainweb.org/realbread/articles/jul21-bread-science-101/

## COV07 - product in use

Cooling finishes the loaf: steam redistributes, crumb firms, crust
texture settles; slicing too early gives a gummy crumb. Storage slows
or speeds staling (starch retrogradation) - mechanism reserved for 02a
confirmation before script use. Sources:
https://www.sustainweb.org/realbread/articles/jul21-bread-science-101/
